Objectives of the Study
The study primarily aims to develop an algorithm that extends the ability of content-generation with mesh-level automated trajectory generation in the setting of acoustic levitation for manipulating particles mid-air using ultrasonic Phased Array of Transducers (PATs). This system is targeted to be used primarily in an educational setting, particularly in laying the groundwork for computer engineering laboratory courses.  
Specifically the study aims:
- To design and construct a functional 16×16 multi-FPGA Acoustic Volumetric Display (AVD) capable of generating stable ultrasonic pressure fields for mid-air particle levitation.
- To develop the software pipeline to be integrated in Blender as an Add-on, including the Automated Trajectory Generation (ATG), for converting 3D model inputs into smooth trajectories and solver-ready phase maps for multi-particle PoV content on AVD systems.

Scope and Limitation
The scope of this study covers the development and evaluation of an ultrasonic Acoustic Volumetric Display (AVD) system designed for use in engineering education. It focuses on constructing a 16×16 phased-array transducer platform driven by synchronized FPGA modules and creating the software pipeline responsible for generating solver-based phase maps and controlling particle levitation. The study also involves implementing the Automated Trajectory Generation (ATG)  to produce particle trajectories and integrating these trajectories with four acoustic field solvers for performance benchmarking. 
The limitations of this study stem primarily from the technical and operational constraints of the developed system. First, the platform accepts only Wavefront (.obj), STL (.stl), and glTF (.gltf) files as input, restricting compatibility with other 3D model formats and requiring users to convert their files before use. The system is also designed specifically for use within select Computer Engineering courses, particularly Embedded Systems (EMBL01E), Computer Engineering Drafting and Design (CDDL01E), and Hardware Description Language (HDLL01E), which limits its generalizability to broader academic or industrial contexts. The physical implementation of the acoustic levitation system is limited to 16 controllable levitated points, a constraint imposed by the capabilities of the Temporal Weighted Gerchberg–Saxton (TWGS) solver described in the Christopoulos et al. (2024), which will be the default solver. As a result, more high-resolution 3D volumetric trajectories cannot be rendered within the current system.

To test and validate the proposed AVD system for both Complex and Simple Shape Volumetric Renders, each with performance metrics such as: (a) Trap Stability, (b) Solver Efficiency, (c) Hardware Synchronization, (d) Trajectory Fidelity, and (e) Overall System Responsiveness.
To evaluate the acceptability of the output system of the proposed study based on the following criteria of ISO/IEC 25010: (a) Performance, (b) Accuracy, (c) Stability, (d) Usability, (e) Educational Feedback.
Significance of the Study
This study is expected to be significant on multiple levels, benefiting various stakeholders and contributing to both practical applications and scholarly knowledge:
Lyceum of the Philippines University – Cavite (LPU Cavite), College of Engineering and Architecture (CEA):
This study will serve as a foundational basis for the future development of classroom visualization tools within LPU Cavite, particularly within the College of Engineering and Architecture. The proposed automated trajectory generation system for acoustic volumetric displays may be integrated into laboratory demonstrations, instructional modules, and course enhancements for subjects such as Embedded Systems (EMBL01E and EMBL02E), Computer Engineering Drafting and Design (CDDL01E), and Hardware Description Language (HDLL01E). The project supports the university’s goal of advancing innovation in engineering education through immersive and technology-driven learning tools.
Engineering Researchers:
Engineering researchers, particularly those focusing on embedded systems, signal processing, visualization technologies, and human–computer interaction, will benefit from the system’s ability to generate real-time, physically consistent 3D visualizations. The automated trajectory generation approach provides a practical platform for analyzing acoustic manipulation, conducting experimental studies, and evaluating solver performance. By offering a hands-on research environment, the system supports deeper technical inquiry and enables researchers to prototype, test, and refine scientific concepts related to acoustic levitation and volumetric display systems.
Future Researchers and the Academic Community:
This study provides a foundational reference for researchers and scholars interested in acoustic holography, trajectory optimization, solver-aware rendering, and educational visualization technologies. The documented framework and validated prototype may be used as a basis for future studies involving advanced particle control, higher-fidelity volumetric rendering, machine learning–assisted solvers, and alternative AVD hardware architectures, contributing to the continued growth of research in volumetric display systems.
